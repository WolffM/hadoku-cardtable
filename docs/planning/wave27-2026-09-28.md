## §A — The table fits 960, and cards find their place

Two jobs, one file set, because both are "how cards sit on the table" and
splitting them across lanes is what deferred placement twice.

**Fit.** At a 1280 viewport the table is 960 wide, and the committed screenshot
`docs/planning/wave26-question-panel-1280x800.png` shows what that costs: at
least one battlefield row compresses its cards until their names overlap and are
unreadable. The row-zone sizing maths already exists (`board.css`, `--card-w`
computed against `--card-max` and the container query) — it is the budget that is
wrong, because the rail, the controls and the log button now take a right gutter
the sizing does not know about.

1. Make a row's card budget account for the reserved right gutter, so cards
   shrink to a legible floor and the row scrolls or wraps rather than crushing.
   **A card whose name cannot be read is a bug**, and that is the acceptance test.
2. Keep `board.css:288`'s rule: every track is a named size or an `fr`. An `auto`
   in that grid lets a card, a flag or a count move a sibling zone.

**Placement.** The operator's model, exactly:

3. **A card entering a zone takes the next free slot.** The player never tidies,
   and nothing overlaps unless they ask. This replaces wave 24's free placement
   as the default; the arrangement STORE stays — a slot is an arrangement.
4. **A card dropped ONTO another stacks with a slight offset**, rendered the way
   an attachment already renders. That is the operator's own comparison, so reuse
   that rendering rather than inventing a second overlap style.
5. The gesture must distinguish "dropped on empty space" from "dropped on a
   card". `gestures/` is yours, so the drop target is yours to report.
6. **Rearranging is not an act.** It sends nothing to Forge. A press that starts
   an arrangement must be claimable with no offer open — `install.ts` bails when
   `match.selectables === null`, and wave 25 §E already had to solve this for the
   hand. Do NOT widen what counts as an OFFER to get there: widening the offer is
   how a click on a card Forge never named becomes an act.

7. **While you are in `Board.tsx`:** `:756` still rewrites `{T}` to the literal
   word `TAP` in rules text, while `ManaPip` now knows `T: 'tap'` and `CardTile`
   renders `ms-tap` (D20). Delete the replace so the symbol shows.
8. Done when: `pnpm check` green; `Board.test.ts` proves a card takes the next
   free slot, a card dropped on a card stacks with an offset, and a full row keeps
   its names legible at the 960 budget — each failing when reverted, with counts;
   and `combat-drag.jvm.test.ts` and `board-play.jvm.test.ts` still pass against
   the real jar, **one file at a time**. Say which measured boxes you moved.

## §F — The app makes a sound

Nothing in this repo plays audio: `Audio`, `AudioContext`, `playSound`, `.mp3`,
`.ogg` return **zero** hits. Greenfield, and the operator's list is *"lots of ui
sounds like hovering, dragging, selecting buttons etc."*

**The seam is the deliverable; the cues are the proof it works.**

1. **One place plays a cue.** A module under `apps/ui/src/sound/` that owns the
   `AudioContext`, a named cue vocabulary (`hover`, `select`, `drag`, `drop`,
   `deny`), and nothing else. No component constructs audio. This is the same
   discipline that kept card art to one resolver and one `<img>`, and the reason
   is the same: twelve call sites cannot be re-pointed later.
2. **Synthesised now, files later.** Each cue is a short WebAudio envelope, and
   the module resolves a cue to a file when one exists — a lookup, like the art.
   The operator will replace every one of these; make that a data change.
3. **Sound is off until a gesture, and remembered.** Browsers refuse audio before
   an interaction, so the context starts suspended and resumes on the first real
   click. Give the player a mute they can find, and remember it per viewer in
   `localStorage` wrapped in try/catch — a blocked store must not break the page.
4. **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`'s cousin:** do not play cues when the OS
   asks for reduced motion is NOT the rule — that pref is about motion. There is
   no standard "reduce sound" query, so the MUTE is the control. Default: on, at
   a low volume.
5. Wire the cues on the surfaces you own — `CardTile` (hover, select) and
   `RoomList` (button press). **The board's own cues are not this wave**: every
   one of them attaches to a file §A or §B is rewriting, and two lanes in one
   file is what this protocol prevents. The next wave wires them, and your API is
   what it will use — so design it for that.
6. Done when: `pnpm check` green; a test proves a cue is requested on hover and
   on select without constructing an `AudioContext` in the test environment
   (inject the sink), and that mute silences it — failing when reverted; and the
   note states the cue vocabulary the next wave will wire the board against.
