## §0b — THE RULE THIS WAVE EXISTS UNDER: stop hand-rolling the shortcuts

The operator, 2026-09-27, and it outranks every item below:

> *"it's super important that we dont bandaid fix these things, especially the
> shortcutting logic. this is gonna keep stacking higher there will continue
> to be more shortcutting logic tweaks to make and its gonna get super messy
> if we handroll too much of this in custom if statements."*

They are right, and the code is worse than "custom if statements".
**`apps/server/src/play/auto.ts` decides whether to interrupt the player by
running eleven regexes over Forge's human-readable prompt text:**

```
/^Priority: ([^\n]*)/      /^Stack: Empty$/m       /^Pay Mana Cost: (.+)$/m
/^Turn: \d+ \(([^)]*)\)/m  /^Phase: Main phase/m   /^Click on your life total to pay life for /m
```

Every shortcut the operator has asked for so far became another pattern and
another branch. Three more arrived today (§E.1's list). The failure mode is
specific: a Forge string change breaks the shortcut silently — no type error,
no test failure unless a fixture happens to carry that exact sentence.

**Forge already solves this, in our own fork.**
`forge-gui/.../gamemodes/match/YieldController.java` is a structured stop
policy: `isSkippingPhase(player, phase)` / `setSkipPhase(...)`,
`autoPassUntilStackEmpty()`, `autoPassUntilEndOfTurn()`, `YieldMarker`,
`shouldAutoYield(key)` / `setShouldAutoYield(key, autoYield, abilityScope)`,
`getDeclineScope(FPref)` — driven by preferences, keyed by ability, and the
same model Forge's own desktop client stops on. The wire already models the
two methods that carry it: `applyYieldUpdate` (S→C, `tier-b.ts:308`) and
`sendYieldUpdate` (C→S) — **and we have never implemented either.**

**So: §E replaces the guessing with Forge's own model, and after it, a new
shortcut is a PREFERENCE, not a branch.** No lane in this wave may add a
regex over prompt text, and no lane may add a special case to `auto.ts`.
If a shortcut cannot be expressed through the yield model, that is a finding
to report — not a licence to hand-roll one more.

## §1 — Settled, do not re-open

- **We implement no rules.** Forge decides legality; the UI renders its answer.
  Arena's *feel* is the target, never Arena's rules engine.
- **Hidden information is absolute.** §D touches reveals and library views —
  nothing may render a card the viewer's seat was not sent. A reveal shown to
  both players is shown because **Forge sent it to both**, never because the
  UI decided to share.
- **The canvas stays static** (w19, absolute). §A changes what is INSIDE the
  battlefield's slot; the slot's box does not move, and
  `e2e/table-fit.spec.ts` stays the proof.
- **No prose; nothing chosen by a number; no WotC marks.** §A.3 draws numbers
  and colours — colour alone may not carry meaning (accessibility), so the
  buffed/damaged states need a second signal.
- **Never build in `forge/`**; never initialise or deinit the submodule.

## §A — the battlefield is the player's

1. **Free placement and stacking** (#1). `rowFor()`'s automatic sorting becomes
   a DEFAULT, not a cage: the player may drop a permanent anywhere on their own
   battlefield and may stack cards on one another. Their arrangement is theirs
   — it sends nothing to the engine, survives a resync, and a "tidy" control
   puts the default grouping back. **Only the viewer's own battlefield is
   arrangeable**, as today.
2. **Equipment renders stacked with what it equips** (#11). Read
   `AttachedCards` / `EntityAttachedTo` in `adapter.ts` — they are already on
   the wire and we ignore them — and draw an equipped creature and its gear as
   one stack. This is Forge's truth, not the player's arrangement, so it must
   not be lost when they rearrange.
3. **P/T tells the truth** (#12). The operator's spec: a 1/2 with +2/+2 that
   has taken 3 shows **3 (blue) / 1 (red)** — blue means buffed, red means
   hurt. `adapter.ts` already reads power, toughness and damage.
   **The open question is printed-vs-current**, and the lane must answer it
   before drawing: Forge's `Power`/`Toughness` are the CURRENT values, so
   "is it buffed" needs the printed base — from our own card data, or from
   Forge's base state. Establish which, from a dump, then draw.
   **Colour may not be the only signal** (accessibility): pair it with shape,
   weight or a marker.

4. **Haste is not summoning sickness** (#17). `adapter.ts:348` sets
   `summoningSick` from Forge's `Sickness`, which is true for anything that
   arrived this turn — haste or not — so a hasty creature wears a chip saying
   it cannot act while it attacks. Find what the wire carries for haste
   (keywords on the current state; `TrackableProperty` has no `Haste` of its
   own) and stop showing the chip when the creature can act. If the wire does
   not carry it, say so rather than inferring haste from card text.
5. **Stacking is deliberate, not just free** (#19): a player may place cards
   anywhere on their own battlefield AND stack them above or below one another
   in an order they choose. Forge's own client has `UI_STACK_CREATURES` and
   `UI_MAX_STACK_DEPTH` as preferences — read them for the shape of the idea,
   but the rendering is ours.

Done when: a player can arrange and stack their own battlefield in a chosen
order and it survives a resync; a haste creature does not claim to be
summoning sick; an equipped creature draws as one stack from Forge's own
attachment data; P/T shows buffed and damaged distinctly with a non-colour
signal too; the static-canvas proof is green at both sizes.

## §B — the pointer: attack, block, target

1. **Selecting an attacker marks it immediately** (#7) — the operator says
   "tap them", and the intent is the mark at SELECTION rather than after
   Forge's declaration lands. Render the intent; Forge's actual tapped state
   remains the truth and must not be faked when they disagree.
2. **Box-draw multi-select** (#7) for declaring attackers, as Arena does.
3. **Blocking by drag** (#13): drag a blocker onto an attacker.
4. **The targeting arrow draws for CLICK targeting too** (#8). The arrow
   exists (wave 20) and is anchored to drag; choosing a target by clicking
   draws nothing. Same arrow, same overlay, both gestures.

All four go through the existing `selectCard` / `selectPlayer` senders — **no
new wire methods**, and Forge remains the authority on what is legal.

Done when: attackers can be declared by box-draw and show their intent at
selection; a blocker is assigned by dragging it onto an attacker; the arrow
draws for click-targeting; and every test asserts what went on the WIRE, not
what was drawn.

## §C — the frame gets out of the way

1. **The control bar stops being a bar** (#5, and the operator's second pass at
   the Leave placement). They accept its contents and reject its shape: let the
   controls sit **over** the board, with full control in the bottom-right
   corner. Reclaim the height for the table. The inspector already owns an
   overlay layer — read it and do not fight it.
2. **A vertical phase indicator** (#6), Arena-shaped: which phase, which step,
   whose turn, read at a glance down the side. The view already carries it.
3. **A prompt belongs where the player is looking** (#21). Teetering Peaks'
   ask appeared as text at the bottom of the screen; it should be centred, and
   while it wants a target the pointer should carry the arrow (§B owns the
   arrow itself — C owns where the prompt sits and B draws into the overlay).
4. **Concede** (#14). `concede` is already on the wire and has never had a
   button. It is destructive and irreversible: confirm before sending, and say
   what it does in as few words as the rest of the UI.

Done when: no full-width bar remains, the phase is readable vertically at a
glance, concede exists behind a confirmation and reaches the wire, and the
canvas boxes are untouched (lane A's static proof still green).

## §D — prompts that do not nag

**Lane E owns WHETHER a prompt is shown. This lane owns what it looks like
when it is.** Do not add auto-answer logic here; if an item below turns out to
be "this should not have been asked at all", hand it to E rather than
answering it in a component.

1. **A cost that is the only way is not a question** (#2). Scalding Tarn's
   *"pay 1 life?"* arrives because Forge asks before charging. **Diagnose it
   from a `CARDTABLE_PLAY_DUMP` first** — which ask, which fields — then
   decide: if the player has already chosen to activate and the cost is
   mandatory, answer it. **If declining is a real choice Forge offers, keep
   the prompt.** Do not guess; the difference is in the frame.
2. **A library search shows what is choosable first** (#3): valid targets at
   the top, the rest present but visibly not selectable. Forge already tells
   us which are choosable.
3. **Those cards have art** (#9) — the "looking at cards in your library" view
   renders names; it should use the same art ids the board does.
4. **The zoom preview belongs above the search view** (#4), not behind it.
5. **A reveal shows both players at once and needs no OK** (#10) — *unless*
   the seat has something to respond with, or full control is on. Same rule as
   w20's auto-answer, applied to reveals. **The hidden-information rule
   governs**: a reveal reaches the opponent only because Forge sent it to
   them.

Done when: an activation whose cost is mandatory asks nothing (or the lane
states why Forge's frame means it must ask); a library search sorts and greys
by Forge's own answer and draws art; the preview sits above the search; and a
reveal needs no acknowledgement unless the seat can act.

## §E — the shortcut engine stops guessing from English

**This lane's deliverable is a MODEL, not a set of answers.** Judge it by
whether the next shortcut request is a preference; if it is another branch,
the lane has failed however well the three items below behave.

1. **Three requests today, and they are one question.** Evolving Wilds asking
   whether to sacrifice (#16); stopping at every priority point when there is
   something to do (#20); Stoneforge waiting for a click on Plains when only
   one payment exists (#22). Each is *"should the player be interrupted
   here?"* — the same question w20 and w22 answered twice with new regexes.
2. **Implement the two wire methods that already exist in the model.**
   `applyYieldUpdate` (S→C, `tier-b.ts:308`) and `sendYieldUpdate` (C→S) are
   specified and unimplemented. They are how a client tells Forge what it
   wants to stop for.
3. **Drive `YieldController`, do not reimplement it.**
   `forge-gui/.../gamemodes/match/YieldController.java` already offers
   per-phase skipping (`setSkipPhase`), auto-pass until the stack empties or
   the turn ends, yield markers, and per-ability auto-yield keyed by ability
   (`setShouldAutoYield(key, autoYield, abilityScope)`). The player's stop
   preferences become Forge's state, and **Forge decides when to stop** —
   which is what the desktop client does and what makes this stop growing.
4. **Retire the regexes.** The eleven patterns in `auto.ts` are the band-aid;
   when the model carries the decisions, they go. If some residue must stay
   as a translation layer, it is a NAMED, tested adapter between structured
   facts and the yield model — never a place to add "one more case".
5. **Full control keeps its meaning**: it clears the yields, so every stop
   comes back. It is the escape hatch and it must remain exact.
6. **What must NOT be shortcut**, and the lane proves each with a test: a stop
   where the seat has a legal action AND something is at stake; a cost whose
   payment is a genuine choice (declining is a real option Forge offers); and
   anything at all while full control is on. **When in doubt the player is
   asked** — a stop they did not want is an annoyance, a stop they did not get
   is a lost game, and that asymmetry is the whole policy.

**Diagnose each of #16, #20 and #22 from a `CARDTABLE_PLAY_DUMP` before
changing behaviour.** Evolving Wilds in particular: establish whether Forge is
offering a real decline or merely confirming a cost the player already
committed to. The two look identical from the prompt text — which is precisely
the reason text is the wrong thing to be reading.

Done when: the three items behave as the operator asked; the decisions are
expressed as yield/preference state rather than string matching; `auto.ts` no
longer pattern-matches prompt prose; full control restores every stop; and the
closing note states **how the next shortcut request would be satisfied** —
naming the preference, not the code path.
