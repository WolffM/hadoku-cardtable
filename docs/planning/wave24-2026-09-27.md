## §1 — Settled, do not re-open

- **We implement no rules.** Forge decides legality; the UI renders its answer.
  Arena's *feel* is the target, never Arena's rules engine.
- **Hidden information is absolute.** §D touches reveals and library views —
  nothing may render a card the viewer's seat was not sent. A reveal shown to
  both players is shown because **Forge sent it to both**, never because the
  UI decided to share.
- **The canvas stays static** (w19, absolute). §A changes what is INSIDE the
  battlefield's slot; the slot's box does not move, and
  `e2e/table-fit.spec.ts` stays the proof.
- **No prose; nothing chosen by a number; no WotC marks.** §A.3 draws numbers
  and colours — colour alone may not carry meaning (accessibility), so the
  buffed/damaged states need a second signal.
- **Never build in `forge/`**; never initialise or deinit the submodule.

### The contract B and C share, settled here

Wave 20 established it and it holds: **A owns markup and CSS and guarantees
`data-card-id`, `data-player-id`, `data-board-row` and a positioned overlay
root; B draws into that root and attaches pointer handlers by delegation; C
owns the frame around the canvas and never reaches inside it.** B never edits
`Board.tsx` or `board.css`; A never adds a pointer handler; C never moves a
card.

## §A — the battlefield is the player's

1. **Free placement and stacking** (#1). `rowFor()`'s automatic sorting becomes
   a DEFAULT, not a cage: the player may drop a permanent anywhere on their own
   battlefield and may stack cards on one another. Their arrangement is theirs
   — it sends nothing to the engine, survives a resync, and a "tidy" control
   puts the default grouping back. **Only the viewer's own battlefield is
   arrangeable**, as today.
2. **Equipment renders stacked with what it equips** (#11). Read
   `AttachedCards` / `EntityAttachedTo` in `adapter.ts` — they are already on
   the wire and we ignore them — and draw an equipped creature and its gear as
   one stack. This is Forge's truth, not the player's arrangement, so it must
   not be lost when they rearrange.
3. **P/T tells the truth** (#12). The operator's spec: a 1/2 with +2/+2 that
   has taken 3 shows **3 (blue) / 1 (red)** — blue means buffed, red means
   hurt. `adapter.ts` already reads power, toughness and damage.
   **The open question is printed-vs-current**, and the lane must answer it
   before drawing: Forge's `Power`/`Toughness` are the CURRENT values, so
   "is it buffed" needs the printed base — from our own card data, or from
   Forge's base state. Establish which, from a dump, then draw.
   **Colour may not be the only signal** (accessibility): pair it with shape,
   weight or a marker.

Done when: a player can arrange and stack their own battlefield and it
survives a resync; an equipped creature draws as one stack from Forge's own
attachment data; P/T shows buffed and damaged distinctly with a non-colour
signal too; the static-canvas proof is green at both sizes.

## §B — the pointer: attack, block, target

1. **Selecting an attacker marks it immediately** (#7) — the operator says
   "tap them", and the intent is the mark at SELECTION rather than after
   Forge's declaration lands. Render the intent; Forge's actual tapped state
   remains the truth and must not be faked when they disagree.
2. **Box-draw multi-select** (#7) for declaring attackers, as Arena does.
3. **Blocking by drag** (#13): drag a blocker onto an attacker.
4. **The targeting arrow draws for CLICK targeting too** (#8). The arrow
   exists (wave 20) and is anchored to drag; choosing a target by clicking
   draws nothing. Same arrow, same overlay, both gestures.

All four go through the existing `selectCard` / `selectPlayer` senders — **no
new wire methods**, and Forge remains the authority on what is legal.

Done when: attackers can be declared by box-draw and show their intent at
selection; a blocker is assigned by dragging it onto an attacker; the arrow
draws for click-targeting; and every test asserts what went on the WIRE, not
what was drawn.

## §C — the frame gets out of the way

1. **The control bar stops being a bar** (#5, and the operator's second pass at
   the Leave placement). They accept its contents and reject its shape: let the
   controls sit **over** the board, with full control in the bottom-right
   corner. Reclaim the height for the table. The inspector already owns an
   overlay layer — read it and do not fight it.
2. **A vertical phase indicator** (#6), Arena-shaped: which phase, which step,
   whose turn, read at a glance down the side. The view already carries it.
3. **Concede** (#14). `concede` is already on the wire and has never had a
   button. It is destructive and irreversible: confirm before sending, and say
   what it does in as few words as the rest of the UI.

Done when: no full-width bar remains, the phase is readable vertically at a
glance, concede exists behind a confirmation and reaches the wire, and the
canvas boxes are untouched (lane A's static proof still green).

## §D — prompts that do not nag

1. **A cost that is the only way is not a question** (#2). Scalding Tarn's
   *"pay 1 life?"* arrives because Forge asks before charging. **Diagnose it
   from a `CARDTABLE_PLAY_DUMP` first** — which ask, which fields — then
   decide: if the player has already chosen to activate and the cost is
   mandatory, answer it. **If declining is a real choice Forge offers, keep
   the prompt.** Do not guess; the difference is in the frame.
2. **A library search shows what is choosable first** (#3): valid targets at
   the top, the rest present but visibly not selectable. Forge already tells
   us which are choosable.
3. **Those cards have art** (#9) — the "looking at cards in your library" view
   renders names; it should use the same art ids the board does.
4. **The zoom preview belongs above the search view** (#4), not behind it.
5. **A reveal shows both players at once and needs no OK** (#10) — *unless*
   the seat has something to respond with, or full control is on. Same rule as
   w20's auto-answer, applied to reveals. **The hidden-information rule
   governs**: a reveal reaches the opponent only because Forge sent it to
   them.

Done when: an activation whose cost is mandatory asks nothing (or the lane
states why Forge's frame means it must ask); a library search sorts and greys
by Forge's own answer and draws art; the preview sits above the search; and a
reveal needs no acknowledgement unless the seat can act.
