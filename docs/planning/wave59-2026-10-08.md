## Settled — do not re-open in any lane

- **The seven rules, as approved.** (1) No stack gallery, no list. (2) A cast card stays where it was
  released: face up for its owner; for the opponent a BACK at the caster's release point until the spell
  is committed to the stack, then its face. Both screens show it at the same place. (3) Its owner can drag
  it while it is on the stack; the move crosses to the other screen; arrows follow it. (4) Each placed
  spell draws an arrow to each target (a card, a player, a spell on the stack); the arrow is live. (5) The
  next item to resolve flashes with the End Turn flash (`live.css:524`, `live-ok-pulse`) — one at a time.
  (6) "Resolve {cardname}?", never a bare "Resolve?". (7) Nothing leaves until something resolves: when
  Remand resolves, Remand and Hinder leave, the flash moves to Keiga, Keiga stays put until it resolves
  into the column it was placed in.
- **The three decisions** (operator, 2026-10-08): (a) a spell cast by a CLICK from the hand (no release
  point) takes a default spot: middle-right of the board; (b) the opponent's card is a back at their
  release point until committed; (c) a spell that resolves to the graveyard fades briefly; with several
  things on the stack they resolve one at a time with a small delay each — a chain reaction. Fast Mode
  keeps its meaning (no pacing delays): in Fast Mode the chain collapses, the fade stays.
- **An activated ability or a trigger on the stack is an EPHEMERAL CARD** (the operator, 2026-10-08:
  *"since we're removing the gallery stack we will need a way to depict when activated abilities go on
  the stack. maybe we can put an ephemeral card somewhere?"*): a card-sized tile, `[data-placed-spell]`
  like a spell, drawn with the source's name and art and the ability's rules text, placed BESIDE its
  source when the source is on the battlefield (to its right, overlapping nothing: the next free spot
  along the row's edge) and at the default spot otherwise. It flashes, draws its arrows and fades like a
  spell. This replaces the source's pulse (`LoneAbilityContext`, `CardOnBoard`'s `data-stack-source`),
  which S deletes.
- **The seam between S and W:** S exports from a new `apps/ui/src/play/live/placed.ts`
  `placeSpell(id, at: Point, side: 'you' | 'them')`, `moveSpell(id, at)`, `commitSpell(id)` (the back
  turns over), `onSpellMoved(listener)`; W calls them and never draws. Points are CLIENT coordinates on
  the caller's side; W converts wire spots with `presence.ts`'s own `placeSpot` / `spotOf`.
- **Hidden information stays hidden.** Nothing on the wire names a card before it is on the stack: the
  release point and a move travel as board spots (`PresenceSpotSchema`), never with a card id; the
  opponent's screen keys the back by "the next spell of theirs to appear" (the AC8 pattern,
  `expectPlacement`). `play.spec`'s isolation check stays green.
- **The arrow's geometry is `gestures/arrow.ts`'s `aimAt(from, to, { neighbours })`** (ledger AA6's
  centre rule): no second arrow module.
- **Theme tokens only. No explanatory UI text.** The flash is the existing keyframes; the fade is a
  transition on the placed element; a number or a word is never added to say what the stack order is —
  the operator, 2026-10-08: *"don't add extra 'next to resolve' text, that's ugly. allow the flashing
  card and arrow, and the 'Resolve {cardname}?' to be the clear indicator for what is next"* (the design
  boards' captions were demo annotations, not UI).
- **Delete what you replace** (`made dead / deleted: …` per closing note): the thumbnail row, the lone
  band, their CSS, `w46-stack.spec.ts`, the `LoneAbilityContext` pulse if S's layer supersedes it (S
  decides and says). Comments explain the code; ids and lane letters go in commit messages only.
- Proof on the claws only: `git -C <worktree> push origin HEAD:refs/heads/verify/w59-<lane>` then
  `gh workflow run forge.yml -R WolffM/hadoku-cardtable --ref verify/w59-<lane> -f only=e2e -f specs='…'`;
  never `--ref main`; delete the branch after; run the e2e tsc in the worktree BEFORE the push. `git -C`
  and absolute paths in every command that commits, pushes or dispatches; never `git add -A`. A plain
  `pnpm exec vitest run <path>` needs no jar env and no lock. Never `heavy.lock`.
- Landing: `scripts/land.sh` from the wave worktree after each verified lane; CI is the gate; closing
  notes landed, then `touch docs/handoff/w59.READY`. Evidence is text under `docs/planning/wave59-evidence/<lane>/`.

## §S — the placed-cards layer (AD4 rules 1–5, 7; AD3; decision a, c's fade)

The caster's own card already stands in for its spell (`carryCastOffStack`); nothing draws the OTHER
seat's spell as a card, and two spells collapse into a thumbnail row. `placed.ts` draws every stack item
as a card at a point, both seats', and the gallery goes.

1. `placed.ts`: for each item of `view.stack` (ids, controller, target ids — `adapter.ts`'s stack item
   shape; read it, do not edit it), an element `[data-placed-spell="<id>"]` in the overlay root at its
   point: the owner's release point (the park, `install.ts`); for a click cast, the default spot
   middle-right of the board (decision a); for an ability or a trigger, the EPHEMERAL CARD beside its
   source (Settled). The item's card tile for a committed spell of either seat; W supplies the
   back-until-committed for theirs.
2. Arrows: for each target of each item, `aimAt(from, to, { neighbours })` from the placed element to the
   target's element — a battlefield card, a player face, another placed spell — redrawn on every view
   change and every move (`onSpellMoved`). `.placed__arrow[data-for="<id>"]`.
3. The next to resolve (the top of Forge's stack) carries `data-placed-next` and the `live-ok-pulse`
   keyframes; exactly one at a time. Hovering a placed spell opens the one inspector with the card's
   text, as hovering a board card does (`w33:485`, `w36:340` read it there now).
4. Resolution: an item that leaves the stack for the battlefield lands in its placed column (the AC8
   reservation, already there); one that leaves for anywhere else fades out over 250 ms (decision c) —
   `[data-placed-leaving]`. Nothing else moves when something resolves.
5. Dragging: a press on `[data-placed-spell]` of the viewer's own moves it (`gestures/install.ts`, the
   same slop and lift as a hand card; no cast, no target); `moveSpell` + `onSpellMoved` for W.
6. AD3: with a spell already on the stack, the second cast's stand-in stays where it was let go: the
   `hover` follow after a park ends at the park (the aim arrow follows the pointer; the card does not).
7. Delete the thumbnail row, the lone band, their CSS and `w46-stack.spec.ts`; adapt the readers in the
   blast-radius table; keep `[data-zone="stack"]` as the non-visual anchor with `data-count`/`data-carried`.
8. Mutation: drop the arrow redraw on move → `placed.test` FAILS; drop the one-at-a-time flash → FAILS.
9. Done when: `placed.test` and `Board.test` fail when reverted; `played-card`, `play`, `w49-counterspell`,
   `drag`, `arrows` green on a claw; `docs/planning/wave59-evidence/S/placed.md` records the DOM contract
   (attributes, the default spot's rule, the fade's timing) and the deleted files.

## §W — the wire and the other screen (AD4 rule 2, 3; decision b)

Today the release carries a column (AC8); a stack spell needs the POINT, and a move of a placed spell
needs to cross too. The opponent's screen draws a back at that point until the spell is committed.

1. `PresenceSchema.placing` gains `at: PresenceSpotSchema` (the release spot on the sender's board —
   `spotOf`, already computed for `from`); a pre-deploy message still parses (optional, default null).
2. New `forge:placed` (`packages/shared/src/placed.ts`): `{ matchId, type: 'placed', spell: <stack item
   id>, at: spot }` — sent when the owner moves a placed spell (`onSpellMoved`); the id is a STACK item's,
   public by then. The server relays it stamped with the seat (as presence) and replays the last one per
   spell to a joiner; the jvm harness ignores it.
3. `presence.ts` receive: on a release with `placing.at`, the back glides to `placeSpot(at)` and waits
   (the AC8 waiting back, at a point instead of a gap); when their spell shows on the stack
   (`theirCards`), `commitSpell(id)` turns it over in place — S draws it from then on; on `forge:placed`,
   `moveSpell(id, placeSpot(at))`.
4. `presence.spec.ts`: bob sees alice's Memnite as a back at her release point, then its face where she
   left it once it is on the stack; alice drags it; bob's copy follows; after bob reloads it is still

6. Done when: `socket.test`, `presence.test` fail when reverted; `presence` green 3/3 on a claw;
