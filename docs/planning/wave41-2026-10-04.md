- **W14** — *"drawing arrows to assign attackers to plainswalker or player is pretty awkward. player doesn't
  show as a valid a target, and after attackers tap a persistent arrow should be showing as to what they are
  attacking."*
- **W5** — *"if i bring something back to my hand and left click it, that should also put it back in the hand."*

## §A

1. **W9.** The arrow snaps to, and a release answers, ONLY what Forge offered for the open question:
   `match.selectables` (cards and players) — never any player face or card under the pointer. A release
   on anything else sends nothing. Prove with a creature-only removal (Grasp of Darkness or Doom Blade): no
   snap to either avatar, no `selectPlayer` sent.
2. **W17.** The arrow's tail starts at its source's element; when the source is a stack item, the stack
   item's element; never a fallback point like (0,0). Prove with a cast trigger that targets (Worldbreaker's
   cast trigger, or any "when you cast" targeting trigger).
3. **W14.** Declaring attackers by arrow: the defending player and each planeswalker are valid drop targets
   and highlight as such; after attackers are declared, a persistent arrow from each attacker to what it
   attacks stays drawn until combat ends. Prove with a scenario that has an opposing planeswalker.
4. **W5.** A card lifted, brought back over the hand, then left-clicked returns to the hand and nothing is
   sent. Prove it.
5. Unit tests per rule (`gestures.test.ts`); mutation-test W9 (snap to any face → its test fails).
6. **Done when:** `pnpm run check` exits 0; each rule's real-Forge script result is in the evidence dir;
   `e2e/arrows.spec.ts` passes 3 runs in a row with the jar env; the report quotes the operator's W9, W17,
   W14, W5 sentences and carries `made dead / deleted`.
