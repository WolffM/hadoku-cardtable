## Settled — do not re-open

- **The same selection strategy as wave 34**, unchanged. `docs/planning/deck-selection/review/README.md` holds its
  rules, and `tools/*.py` implement them:
  - one "power summer" era per year;
  - candidates are every deck from a competitive-level event on mtgtop8 (Pro Tour, Worlds, GP, Nationals, SCG
    Opens, PTQs; not online dailies) in the season, with summer events weighted higher;
  - legal card by card against **Forge's own dated format file** (sets + bans) for that date;
  - near-duplicate archetypes rejected;
  - **12 decks per era where the season has them**, spread across colours and styles (aggro / midrange / control
    / combo);
- **Balance, but true to the season** (operator, 2026-10-05: *"make sure they have a good balance of aggro, combo,
  control, mid-range. and also good color balance. but don't overdo it. for example, if white was very
  overrepresented in one year then its okay to have a couple white decks in there. same with deck archetypes,
  etc."*):
  - every era aims for all five colours and all four styles;
  - a colour or style that DOMINATED that season's meta may take an extra slot or two. The evidence is its
    share of the era's candidates, and the review doc says so in one line;
  - never force a weak, unrepresentative deck in just to fill a colour or style the season barely played.
    A missing style is reported, not invented. Wave 34's 2014 (no combo) and 2016 (no combo) are the precedent;
  - each era's review doc shows the candidate pool's colour and style shares beside the chosen twelve's, so the
    balance can be checked against the meta;
  - every deck cites its mtgtop8 URL;
  - a deck Forge cannot transcribe is swapped for the next alternate in its era, recorded with
    `swapped_out_reason`.
- **The eras** (tracker's defaults; the operator may override):
  - **Standard 2003–2007**: five more power summers, found by `eras.py`'s existing rule (the last Standard
    snapshot before each fall rotation).
  - **Modern 2012–2016**: Modern was created in August 2011, so 2012 is its first summer. Forge's
    `Archived/Modern/2011-*` files predate the format's play and are not an era.
  - **Legacy 2005–2016**: Legacy began in September 2004, so 2005 is its first summer.
- **Modern and Legacy eras line up with Standard's.** These formats don't rotate, so for year Y:
  - the snapshot date is that year's Standard era `start`;
  - legality is the latest `Archived/<Format>/` file on or before that date;
  - the season window is the same as Standard's (`season_start` → `end`).
  That way a year's three formats sit side by side.
- **Thin seasons are reported, not padded.** If mtgtop8 has fewer legal candidates than 12, the era is smaller,
  as 2009 (4) and 2010 (8) are today. Legacy 2005–2007 and Standard 2003–2004 may be thin; say how many there were.
- **Polite crawling:** the existing on-disk cache (`lists.py`, outside the repo) and a delay between requests.
  Three lanes crawl mtgtop8 at once, so use one shared cache and stop at the first sign of throttling. Never hammer it.
- **Art:** nothing to do. An id with no `art-manifest.json` row falls back to Scryfall
  (`packages/carddata/src/art/index.ts`). Don't add manifest rows, and keep image bytes out of the repo.
- **New rooms still start on the newest STANDARD era.** Today `lobby.ts:994` takes the newest frozen pool,
  meaning the LAST element of the formats list. With Modern and Legacy in the list, "last" must not become
  Legacy 2016 by accident.
- No version bumps and no ledger edits in lanes. The manager does not push; it reports ready, and the tracker
  integrates w46, w47, then w48, and pushes once.
