### Two of these already have a diagnosis — do not re-derive them

**#1 is ours, and it is one line.** `live/match.ts:535`:

```ts
const offered = match.selectables === null ? cardIdsInView(view) : new Set(match.selectables.cards);
```

When Forge has sent no selectables list, the board offers **every card in
view**. The blue outline is invented by our UI, not by the rules engine, so a
click on it reaches a server that has nothing to do with it. Forge tells us
exactly what is clickable through `setSelectables` — when it has not, the
answer is *nothing*, not *everything*.

**#8 is not a rules bug.** Forge auto-taps lands to pay a cost; the spell was
paid for. What is missing is any sign that it happened — no "paid {R}", no
visible tapping at the moment of payment — so a legal cast read as a free one.
§C makes the payment visible. **Do not go looking for an illegal cast**; if a
lane finds evidence of one, it stops and reports rather than "fixing" the
rules, which are Forge's and not ours.

## Settled — do not re-open in any lane

- **The canvas is static. This is absolute.** No zone appearing, filling or
  emptying may change the size or position of any other zone. The battlefield
  does not move because the stack got an item. Anything that needs more room
  than its slot is a popout, an overlay or a scroll *inside its own slot* —
  never a reflow. W17's "the table never scrolls" still holds: 1280×800 and
  1366×768, no zone scrollbars in normal play.
- **We implement no rules.** Forge decides what is legal; the UI renders
  Forge's answer. A card is clickable because `setSelectables` said so.
- **The hidden-information rule is absolute.** Nothing may render a card the
  viewer's seat was not sent.
- **No WotC trademarks** — no mana symbols, set symbols, tap symbol, card
  frames, wordmark. Draw our own. This covers the new player faces and the
  targeter arrow.
- **A card's art is a lookup, not a URL** (D15). No component hardcodes a host.
- **No prose in the UI** (operator, 2026-09-24). Labels and states, never
  explanations. An empty zone says nothing at all rather than "empty".
- **Never build in `forge/`**; never initialise the submodule in a worktree.

### The two contracts, settled here rather than negotiated mid-wave

**A ↔ B — the DOM is the seam.** B draws an arrow between things and drags
cards onto things; it must find them without owning the markup. A guarantees,
and a test in A pins:

- every rendered card carries `data-card-id="<id>"`;
- every player's face carries `data-player-id="<id>"`;
- every zone carries `data-zone="<zone>"` and `data-owner="you|them"`;
- a positioned overlay root exists at the board's top level, `data-board-overlay`,
  covering the canvas exactly, which B renders into.

B never edits markup or `board.css`; A never adds a pointer handler.

**D → A/B — the card model gains an art id.** D puts a resolved art id on
every card the adapter emits **and on every stack item** (`fold.ts`), so A can
render stack art without knowing where art comes from. D's field name and shape
are D's to choose; D tells the manager in its first report and the manager
tells A.

## §A — the canvas, and what it looks like

1. **Static canvas.** Lay the board out so every zone's slot is fixed — grid
   areas with fixed tracks, not content-sized ones. The stack has a slot that
   is the same size whether it holds nothing or five items; an item overflows
   *inside* the slot. Prove it: a test that renders empty, one item, and five
   items and asserts the battlefield's box is identical in all three.
2. **Hand and battlefield read as different places** — different backgrounds,
   clearly, at a glance. Ours, not a card frame.
3. **A face per player, in the middle.** The opponent's face and yours,
   centre-ish, with life on them — a real click target, `data-player-id`.
   Arena's affordance, our art. It is a button when B says it is selectable
   (the `selectable` class/attribute A already uses for cards).
4. **No empty-state prose.** "Stack 0 / empty" and every sibling goes. An empty
   zone renders its slot and nothing else.
5. **Stack items draw their card's art** (from D's field) at a size that reads,
   inside the fixed slot.

Done when: the three-render box test passes; `pnpm check` green; the DOM
contract above holds under a test; `e2e/table-fit.spec.ts` still green at both
sizes.

## §B — what is clickable, and how you click it

1. **Only what Forge offered.** Delete the `cardIdsInView` fallback: with
   `selectables === null` the board offers **nothing**. Mutation-test it —
   restore the fallback and the new test must fail. Nothing may glow that a
   click cannot use.
2. **The targeter arrow.** While a selection is live, dragging (or holding)
   from the source draws an arrow to the pointer, and to whatever is under it
   when it snaps to a legal target. Rendered into A's overlay root, from the
   positions of `data-card-id` / `data-player-id`.
3. **Drag a card.** Dragging a card to a legal drop target does what a click
   does — `selectCard`, then the target's `selectCard`/`selectPlayer` — with
   the click path kept working exactly as it does now. A drag that ends
   nowhere legal does nothing and says nothing.
4. Both gestures go through the existing `selectCard`/`selectPlayer` senders.
   No new wire methods.

Done when: nothing is clickable that Forge did not offer (mutation-tested);
arrow and drag are covered by tests that assert what was SENT, not what was
drawn; `e2e/play.spec.ts` drives a land and a spell through **drag** as well
as click; `pnpm check` + `pnpm e2e` green.

## §C — fewer stops, and show what was paid

1. **No mana-colour question with one answer.** When a land or source produces
   exactly one colour, or the cost admits exactly one payment, answer Forge
   immediately instead of asking. Ask only when the choice is real.
2. **Do not stop where nothing can be done.** When the seat has priority, has
   no legal action, and nothing is on the stack, pass — a phase the player
   cannot act in is not a decision. Forge's own yield machinery
   (`sendYieldUpdate`) is the mechanism if it fits; a client-side auto-pass is
   acceptable if it does not, but it must be *observable* (a line saying what
   was auto-passed) and it must never skip a stop where the seat HAS a legal
   action.
3. **A cast must never look free.** When a cost is paid, show it: the lands
   that tapped, and what they paid. This is #8's whole fix.
4. **A "full control" escape hatch**, off by default, that disables 1 and 2 —
   because the one thing worse than a stop you did not want is a stop you did
   not get.
5. **Nothing is chosen by typing a number.** The ability picker (#11) is the
   worst of it: a numbered list is how the harness talked to a human, not how
   a game does. An ability is picked by clicking the ability, on or beside its
   card; a card is picked by clicking the card. The same goes for every prompt
   that currently enumerates — the number may stay as a keyboard shortcut, but
   it is never the affordance.
6. **Look at what Preordain shows (#12).** The operator saw "the whole library
   with all the cards numbered". Preordain looks at two. Establish from a
   `CARDTABLE_PLAY_DUMP` what Forge actually sent that seat before changing a
   line: if the wire carried two cards and we drew a library, it is ours to
   fix; if the wire carried the library, **stop and report** — that is a
   hidden-information question and it outranks the whole wave. Do not "fix" it
   by filtering in the component, which would hide a leak rather than close it.

Done when: a match played end to end in the jvm-harness stops only where a
legal action exists; the auto-answers are unit-tested against the real prompt
fixtures; payment is visible in the board's own state; no prompt requires
reading a number to answer it; the Preordain finding is recorded either as a
fix or as a report; `pnpm check` green with
the jar-gated suites run locally (`CARDTABLE_FORGE_JAR` + `CARDTABLE_FORGE_RES`
+ **`CARDTABLE_PLAY_WORK_DIR` pointed at scratch**).

## §D — every card has art

**The diagnosis, already made:** art resolves from a card's `scryfallId`
(manifest row, else Scryfall). Cards that reach the board without one — basic
lands and anything outside the drafted pool — therefore have no art at all,
which is exactly the set the operator saw blank. `art-manifest.json` holding
three rows is not the bug; the missing ids are.

1. Give every card the adapter emits a `scryfallId` where one can be known.
   Forge names a printing as `name|SET|artIndex` (`adapter.ts:44`); that is
   enough to resolve against Scryfall's own data. Build the mapping as
   committed data (a script under `scripts/art/`, output checked in), not a
   runtime lookup against a third party.
2. The same id reaches stack items through `fold.ts` — that is #10.
3. **No image bytes in the repo or the bundle**, and no copied Scryfall
   `image_uris` blob: the id is the lookup, `art/index.ts` owns the URL.
   `scripts/d13-gate.sh` must stay green and its self-test must still pass.

Done when: a basic land and an out-of-pool card both render art in a test that
resolves through the real resolver; the stack carries the id; the image gate is
green; `pnpm check` green.
