# Retrospective — wave 35 took eleven hours

**Operator, 2026-10-03:** *"it ran for 10hrs. actually 10 hrs. that's a completely unacceptable amount of
time to do any of the things i asked for."* Agreed. This is the tracker's retrospective; the manager's
own, from artifacts, is `wave35-evidence/manager-retro.md` and its timeline is not repeated here.

## It is a trend, not one bad wave

Plan commit → landing commit (git log):

| wave | plan → landed | lanes | items | what it carried |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| w30 | ~1.3 h | 2 | 4 | board default, settings |
| w31 | ~1.3 h | 6 | 14 | incl. one Forge-fork change |
| w32 | ~2.8 h | 6 | 20 | drag rewrite |
| w33 | ~5.3 h | 5 | 8 | scenario harness, "ten green runs" rule introduced |
| w34 | ~3.6 h | 5 | 6 | |
| **w35** | **~11 h** | **7** | **20** | engine change + Arena casting + 18 other items |

Implementation is not what grew: in w35, six of seven lanes were coded and passing their own tests in
**~2.5 hours** (manager's timeline). The other **~8.5 hours were integration and verification**, and
almost every cause traces to how the tracker planned the wave.

## Causes, and whose they are

### Tracker (the planner): five causes, the large majority of the time

1. **Too much in one wave.** 20 items, 7 lanes, the biggest UI rework yet (Arena casting) AND an engine
   change in the same wave. I sized waves by file ownership, not by risk or by integration cost.
2. **I planned file boundaries and missed behaviour boundaries** (≈4.5 h). Lane F changed what Forge
   calls "actionable" (lands became clickable). Four other readers of that one fact changed meaning at
   once: server auto-pass, `nothingToDecide`, the e2e helpers, the scenario pay loops. My plan named
   none of them. My pin check greps for files that mention a file; it cannot see "who depends on this
   behaviour".
3. **My install-the-jar-before-pushing rule (from w31) put production in a broken state for 9.5 h.**
   The operator installed F's jar at 09:59 as the protocol said; the server code that compensates for it
   shipped at 19:42. The operator played a whole game on that mismatch.
4. **My verification rules multiplied wall-clock time on one shared host.** After w32's vacuous passes I
   added "ten consecutive green runs per new spec", "the manager re-runs every claim at least three
   times", and the full CI spec set before pushing. Each is reasonable; together, with real-Forge e2e at
   1–8 minutes a run, serialised behind one lock on one machine, they are hours. I never budgeted them.
5. **One push at the end, so integration happened at the end.** I required a single push because a
   redeploy kills live games (`treekill`, the `hadoku_site` fix that has been open since 2026-09-30).
   That turned every wave into a big-bang merge: E (the e2e lane) could only integrate once everyone
   had landed, and the first full real-Forge run happened around hour six.

### Lanes and manager: send-backs, roughly two hours

Five "done" reports were not done (manager's table): R7 cast at release instead of at the settle; the
Thoughtseize fold hid every unrelated reveal; R9 fixed only board-to-board moves; the "swallowed press"
fix was really a test fix; the rail fix broke a neighbour. Each cost a full round. The common thread:
the lane proved its own reading of the ask, not the operator's words in a real game.

### Environment: roughly 1.5 hours

E was blocked for an hour by another lane's typecheck break that nobody saw; sub-agents were cut off
mid-run by their hand-back timer and resumed; a host memory-pressure episode (another app at 20–40 GB,
38 GiB swap, load 14.7) produced five red full-set runs before one clean one.

## What changes, starting with wave 36

Adopted by the tracker now (they are planning rules, mine to change):

1. **A time budget per wave: 3 hours target, 4 hours hard stop.** At hour 4 the manager lands every lane
   that is verified and moves the rest to the next wave. No wave runs past 4 hours again. The closing
   note reports the wall-clock time per lane.
2. **Scope cap: at most 8 items, at most one long pole, at most 4 lanes.** A wave that does not fit is
   two waves.
3. **An engine change (Forge fork, jar, auto-pass policy) is a wave of its own**, landed and deployed
   before any UI wave builds on it, and it ships **with** the server code that handles it: the jar is
   installed at push time, as one step with the push, never hours before.
4. **Every plan has a "blast radius" section:** for any change to a shared contract (what Forge offers,
   what auto-pass does, a gesture every spec uses), the plan lists every reader of it, found by grep,
   and assigns each one. No more file-only ownership for behaviour.
5. **An integration checkpoint at hour 1:** as soon as the first two lanes commit, the manager builds
   and runs the full real-Forge e2e set once. Breakage is found at hour 1, not hour 6.
6. **A verification budget instead of open-ended reruns:** a new spec needs **3** consecutive green runs
   in the lane and **one** confirming run by the manager; the "ten runs" rule goes. Flakiness is caught
   by the tracker's post-landing re-verification and by CI over the following waves, which is where
   w34's and w35's flakes were actually found.
7. **"Done" means the operator's words, shown.** A lane's report starts with the operator's sentence for
   the item and a proof in a real game (an e2e frame, a real-Forge test), not a reading of it.

Needs the operator (not mine to decide or do):

8. **Fix `treekill` in `hadoku_site`** (`docs/requests/HADOKU_SITE_CARDTABLE_TREEKILL.md`, filed
   2026-09-30, given to a session, never landed; production still shows `treekill: true`). It is why
   waves are one big push. With it fixed, lanes land one at a time as they pass, and a deploy no longer
   ends anybody's game. **This is the single change that would most shorten waves**, and it is also an
   alpha blocker.
9. **Host contention.** The CI throttle and every real-Forge run share one machine with another app that
   holds 20–40 GB. Either cap that app's memory while a wave runs, or accept slower waves.

## How we will know it worked

Wave 36 is planned under rules 1–7. Its closing note reports wall-clock per lane and in total. Target:
landed in under 3 hours, hard-stopped at 4. If it is not, the next retrospective starts from that number.
